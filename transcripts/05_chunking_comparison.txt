RECOMMENDATION
==============================================================================
The two strategies split the axes rather than one dominating: sentence-based scores mean Precision@3 = 0.8000 versus 0.6333 for fixed-size-with-overlap, while fixed-size scores mean Recall@3 = 1.0000 versus 0.8000. The per-query rows show why, and they show that the recall gap is not the win it looks like: fixed-size only reaches perfect recall because its 420-character windows straddle document boundaries and sweep extra parent documents into R indiscriminately - the same behaviour that costs it precision on Q1, Q2 and Q4, where it drags in a document that has nothing to do with the question. Sentence-based loses recall on exactly and only Q3 and Q5, the two queries whose gold set contains a secondary cross-reference document (kb08_probation_period) rather than the document that actually answers the question; on both it still retrieved the primary document at rank 1.
I would deploy the sentence-based collection. For a support agent the failure that hurts a recruiter is a confidently wrong answer assembled from an unrelated policy, which is a precision failure, and precision is the axis sentence-based wins 0.80 to 0.63. Its chunks also end on sentence boundaries, so every retrieved chunk is a complete, quotable statement of policy, which makes the grounded answer cleaner. The costs are real but small: 36 chunks instead of 24 (a 50% larger index) and the missed cross-reference documents, which I would recover by raising top_k rather than by switching back to a chunker that cuts policies in half.
